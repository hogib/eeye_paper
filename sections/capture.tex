\subsection{Image acquisition}\label{sec:capture}

\eeye{} captures from a USB (UVC) camera through V4L2 \cite{v4l2}
with memory-mapped
streaming, or from a Raspberry Pi camera module through a \texttt{rpicam-vid}
\cite{rpicamapps} child process; a third source reads a frame bus stream
(placement~A). On V4L2 it takes MJPEG when the camera offers it at
the requested size
and packed 4:2:2 (YUYV) otherwise, because at USB~2.0 bandwidth most
cameras offer
720p only as MJPEG. When the requested size is not offered, the
closest mode is chosen
by aspect ratio first and pixel count second, once at startup, since
all frame buffers
are allocated at that size. MJPEG is decoded with libjpeg-turbo
\cite{libjpegturbo}
directly into 4:2:2 planes; an optional downscale by 2, 4 or 8 is
applied inside the
decoder (by dropping DCT coefficients) or, for YUYV, while the packed
samples are
deinterleaved.

Every frame is held as planar I422 at full range (0--255 for luma and
  chroma, the range
the operators assume \cite{itu_bt601}). It carries a frame number and
its capture
time on \texttt{CLOCK\_MONOTONIC}: the driver's buffer timestamp when the driver
stamps that clock, otherwise the time of receipt. Each capture takes,
after the first
ready buffer, every further buffer the driver has filled, returns the older ones
unread and decodes only the newest; when all buffers fill, the V4L2
driver keeps the
oldest frames and drops new ones, and without this a single slow frame left the
picture permanently several frames behind.

Camera controls (exposure, gain, white balance, brightness, contrast,
  saturation,
sharpness) are expressed in device-independent units, percentages of
the device's
reported range or physical units (exposure in microseconds, white
balance in kelvin),
and are mapped onto each camera's own range. A field left out leaves
that control
untouched. One control is set by default: the V4L2 control that lets automatic
exposure lengthen the shutter beyond the frame period is turned off,
because with it
on the laptop camera used for testing
(Section~\ref{sec:verification}) fell from 30 to
17~frames per second in a dim room, and every later stage inherits the rate. In
placement~A the controls travel from the topside to the vehicle's
\hellion{}, which
holds the camera, applies them, and keeps them for its next start.
